Civic-tech for participatory budgeting

Revolutionizing Tax from a scalar to a vector.

Today your tax is a single number handed to the government. #TaxDirection explores how taxpayers could express priorities for a small slice of that amount — turning one scalar into a directed vector of intent, subject to public policy and approval.

Launch the app → See live results

Free and open. Explore with fictional data; real identity verification and government adoption require approved integrations.

The problem

Tax is a scalar today

When you pay tax, you transmit exactly one piece of information: an amount. A magnitude. A scalar. Everything about why you earned it, where you live, and which problems you'd most like solved is discarded at the point of payment.

Governments then make spending decisions with almost no structured, per-taxpayer signal of priority. Citizens, in turn, feel no line of sight between the tax they pay and the outcomes they care about. The feedback loop is broken on both ends.

Tax as a scalar

₹ 1,00,000

One number. No direction. No stated priority.

→

Tax as a vector

  • Clean water for my district40%
  • Local school infrastructure35%
  • Public transit safety25%

Same money — now carrying magnitude and direction.

How it works

Five steps from citizen to proposal

  1. 1

    Onboard

    Open a citizen account or explore a clearly labelled fictional demo. No app installation is needed.

  2. 2

    Verify identity & yearly evidence

    The framework supports country-specific signed verification receipts. The demo uses fictional evidence; real authority connections, including PAN, are not yet enabled.

  3. 3

    Declare your tax

    Tell us the tax you've declared for the assessment year. This sets the pool that your directable fraction is computed from.

  4. 4

    Set your directions

    Describe the problems you want solved, each with a location and percentage. Your shares must total 100% before you confirm and lock the year.

  5. 5

    Follow shared proposals

    Submitted directions enter a batch and one problem–location cell each. Follow source-linked proposal drafts and government responses. Optional AI drafts remain subject to review.

The math

How a wish becomes a rupee amount

The research model uses an illustrative Government-Allowed Percentage as an admin-controlled policy setting (default 5%). No participating government has approved this allowance here; the demo's amounts are advisory priority weights. Within that modelled slice, your direction weights decide the split.

Worked example

  • Declared tax₹ 1,00,000
  • Government-allowed percentage5%
  • Total directable pool₹ 5,000
  • This wish's normalized weight50%

Directed amount ₹ 1,00,000 × 0.05 × 0.50 = ₹ 2,500

So a citizen with ₹1,00,000 of declared tax, under a 5% rule, who places half of their weight on one wish, directs ₹2,500 toward that problem. The remaining weights distribute the rest of the ₹5,000 pool across their other wishes.

Why it matters

Five percent is the gamechanger

A proposed slice of 5–10% offers a policy question to evaluate — the government decides whether to adopt any rule and how large the slice could be. The initial system is advisory. The idea converts tax from a silent transfer into a rich, structured stream of citizen priorities: more data, more insight, and far more accountability, year after year, budget after budget.

“Maybe not 100% of the amount, but say 5% can be allocated to projects of their choice.”

Prof. M. V. Rajeev Gowda Former Member of Parliament, Rajya Sabha (Karnataka, 2014–2020); Professor at IIM Bangalore (2000–2014). Shared while discussing #TaxDirection at Zu~Grama, early 2025.

That conversation reframed the project. #TaxDirection began as a feedback system to tell the government which decisions to make. Allowing even 5% of each person's tax to be allocated to a specific problem they care about turns that feedback into direct, AI-enabled, holistic policy development — viable to pitch in leading democracies as taxpayer-influenced yearly budgeting.
